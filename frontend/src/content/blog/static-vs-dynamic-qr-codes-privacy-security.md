When deploying QR codes on physical items, choosing between **static** and **dynamic** encoding is the most consequential decision you will make.

Many online generators advertise "free dynamic QR codes" that secretly redirect through third-party servers. Weeks after you print thousands of product boxes or business cards, the provider deactivates the codes and demands a $15–$40/month subscription to unlock them.

## 1. Static QR Codes (Self-Contained Data)

- **How It Works:** Your exact URL, Wi-Fi password, or vCard contact is directly translated into the geometric pattern of black and white modules.
- **Dependency: Zero.** No server or company stands between the camera and your data. Once generated and printed, the code functions indefinitely with zero ongoing maintenance.

---

## 2. Dynamic QR Codes (The Middleman Risk)

- **How It Works:** The QR matrix stores a short URL owned by the generator company. When scanned, the user's phone connects to the third-party proxy server, which looks up the destination in a database and redirects the visitor.
- **The Risk:** If you cancel your monthly subscription, or if the provider experiences downtime or goes out of business, your printed QR codes instantly break.

### 1. vCard Digital Business Cards
Personal contact cards should never route through a marketing server. A static **vCard QR code** stores your name, phone, and email locally, opening 1-tap address book import on iOS and Android even with zero cellular reception.

### 2. Wi-Fi Guest Access
Sending your home or corporate Wi-Fi password to a cloud service creates unnecessary security exposure. A static Wi-Fi QR code connects visitors instantly without uploading credentials to remote databases.
