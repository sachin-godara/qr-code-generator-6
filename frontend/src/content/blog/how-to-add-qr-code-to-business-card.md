A QR code on your business card transforms a piece of paper into an interactive digital experience. Instead of manually typing your phone number, email, or website, contacts simply scan and save — instantly, with zero errors.

Here's how to create a professional QR code for your business card, step by step.

## What Should Your Business Card QR Code Link To?

Before designing, decide what happens when someone scans your card. Here are the most popular options:

### Option 1: vCard (Digital Contact Card) ⭐ Recommended

A **vCard QR code** encodes your full contact information — name, phone, email, company, title, website, and address — directly into the QR code. When scanned, the contact info is saved to the phone's address book in one tap.

**Why vCard is best for business cards:**
- Contact info is saved directly — no internet connection needed
- Works offline (data is in the QR code, not on a server)
- Eliminates typos from manual entry
- Supports multiple phone numbers, emails, and URLs

### Option 2: Website URL

Link directly to your personal website, LinkedIn profile, or company page. Best if your online presence is your main calling card.

### Option 3: Portfolio / Landing Page

Create a dedicated landing page (e.g., `yourname.com/card`) with links to your social profiles, portfolio, recent projects, and contact form.

## Step 2: Design Your QR Code to Match Your Brand

A generic black-and-white QR code looks out of place on a carefully designed business card. Here's how to make it look professional:

### Color

- Match the QR code dot color to your brand's primary color
- Use a transparent or white background
- Maintain 4.5:1 contrast ratio for reliable scanning

### Dot Style

- **Rounded dots** — Modern, approachable feel
- **Classic squares** — Clean, professional, tech-oriented
- **Fluid / organic shapes** — Creative, design-forward

### Logo

- Add your company logo to the center of the QR code
- GenerateCustomQR automatically adjusts error correction to Level H
- Use a white padding ring around the logo for clean separation

### Frame (Optional)

- Add a subtle text frame with a CTA like "Scan to Connect" or "Save My Contact"
- Keep the frame text small and legible

## Step 3: Size and Placement on the Card

### Sizing Guide

- **Standard business card:** 8.5 × 5.5 cm (3.5 × 2 in)
- **Recommended QR size:** 2 × 2 cm to 2.5 × 2.5 cm
- **Minimum QR size:** 1.5 × 1.5 cm (any smaller and scanning becomes unreliable)

### Placement Options

| Position | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| **Back center** | Maximum space, clean design | Not visible when card is face-up |
| **Front bottom-right** | Always visible, easy to scan | Takes space from other content |
| **Front bottom-left** | Balanced with right-aligned text | May conflict with logo placement |
| **Back corner** | Leaves room for additional info | Smaller size constraint |

> **Pro Tip:** Place the QR code on the **back of the card** with a brief CTA like "Scan to save my contact." This keeps the front clean and professional while giving the QR code maximum space.

## Step 4: Export for Print

**Always use SVG or PDF format** for business cards:

- **SVG** — Vector format that scales perfectly to any print size
- **PDF** — 300 DPI print-ready, compatible with all print shops
- **PNG** — Use at least 2048px resolution if SVG/PDF isn't supported

## Step 5: Send to Print

When submitting your design to a print shop:

1. **Place the SVG/PDF** QR code in your design software (Canva, Figma, Illustrator, InDesign)
2. **Ensure the quiet zone** (white border around the QR code) is at least 2mm on each side
3. **Set bleed correctly** — Don't let bleed marks encroach on the QR code
4. **Choose matte finish** — Glossy cards can create glare that interferes with scanning
5. **Request a proof** — Print a single copy first and test-scan before ordering the full batch

## Common Mistakes

❌ **Too small** — Under 1.5cm, scanning becomes unreliable, especially with logos

❌ **Low resolution PNG** — A 200px PNG will look pixelated on a professional card. Use SVG.

❌ **Glossy card stock** — Shiny surfaces create reflections that block the camera. Matte or satin finish is safer.

❌ **Encoding too much data** — vCards with extremely long addresses or notes create dense QR patterns. Keep data concise.

❌ **No quiet zone** — Placing the QR code flush against a border or other design element breaks scanning.
