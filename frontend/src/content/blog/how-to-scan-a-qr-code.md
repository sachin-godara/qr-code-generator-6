# How to Scan a QR Code on iPhone and Android

QR codes are everywhere — on restaurant menus, product packaging, event tickets, and business cards. But how do you actually scan one? This guide covers every method across iPhone, Android, and desktop devices.

## Scanning QR Codes on iPhone (iOS 15+)

Apple has built QR code scanning directly into the **Camera app** since iOS 11. No extra app needed.

### Steps:

1. **Open the Camera app** — swipe left from the lock screen or tap the Camera icon.
2. **Point the camera at the QR code** — hold your phone steady about 10–30 cm away.
3. **Tap the notification banner** — a yellow banner will appear at the top with the QR code's content (URL, text, WiFi credentials, etc.).
4. **Done!** — you'll be taken to the linked website, WiFi settings, or contact card automatically.

### Troubleshooting iPhone Scanning

- **No banner appears?** Go to *Settings → Camera* and make sure *Scan QR Codes* is toggled ON.
- **Too far away?** Move closer until the QR code fills about one-third of the screen.
- **Damaged code?** Try the [GenerateCustomQR scanner](/qr-code-scanner) which uses advanced algorithms to read partially damaged codes.

## Scanning QR Codes on Android

Android devices offer several scanning methods depending on your phone brand and Android version.

### Method 1: Google Lens (Recommended)

Google Lens is the most reliable QR scanner on Android:

1. **Open Google Lens** — tap the Lens icon in the Google search bar or the Google app.
2. **Point at the QR code** — Lens will automatically detect and highlight it.
3. **Tap the highlighted area** — the content will be displayed or the URL will open.

### Method 2: Camera App (Samsung, Pixel, OnePlus)

Most modern Android phones (Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel, OnePlus) have a built-in QR scanner in the Camera app:

1. **Open your Camera app.**
2. **Point at the QR code** — a popup or link should appear automatically.
3. **Tap the link** to open it.

> **Samsung users:** If scanning doesn't work, open *Camera Settings* and enable *Scan QR codes*.

### Method 3: Quick Settings Tile

On Android 13+ and Samsung One UI:

1. **Swipe down** from the top of the screen to open Quick Settings.
2. **Tap the QR Scanner tile** (you may need to edit tiles to add it first).
3. **Point and scan** — this method is the fastest since it skips the camera app entirely.

## Scanning QR Codes from a Screenshot or Saved Image

What if the QR code is already saved as an image on your phone? You don't need to print it out!

### On iPhone:
1. Open the **Photos** app and find the image with the QR code.
2. **Long-press the QR code** in the image — iOS will detect it and show options like "Open in Safari."
3. Alternatively, use the **Live Text** feature (iPhone XS and later).

### On Android:
1. Open **Google Lens** and tap the gallery icon.
2. **Select the image** containing the QR code.
3. Google Lens will detect and decode it automatically.

## Common QR Code Scanning Problems and Fixes

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Scanner doesn't recognize the code | Clean your camera lens; ensure good lighting |
| Code is too small | Move closer or zoom in; QR codes under 2cm are hard to scan |
| Low contrast colors | Dark foreground on light background scans best |
| Code is damaged or partially covered | Try a dedicated scanner app with error correction support |
| Blurry camera | Hold your phone steady and let it focus for 1–2 seconds |

## Tips for Creating Scannable QR Codes

If you're creating QR codes for others to scan, keep these best practices in mind:

- **Use Error Correction Level H** when adding a logo — it recovers up to 30% of the code even if partially obscured.
- **Maintain a 4.5:1 contrast ratio** between dots and background for reliable scanning.
- **Keep the quiet zone** (white border) at least 4 modules wide.
- **Test with 3+ scanner apps** before printing large batches.
