If you've ever searched for a QR code generator, you've probably seen the terms "static" and "dynamic" thrown around. But what do they actually mean — and which one should you use?

In this guide, we break down the differences, pros, cons, and ideal use cases for each type.

## Quick Comparison

| Feature | Static QR Code | Dynamic QR Code |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Data storage** | Encoded directly in the image | Redirects through a short URL |
| **Editable after printing** | No | Yes |
| **Scan tracking / analytics** | No | Yes |
| **Expiration** | Never expires | May expire (depends on provider) |
| **Server dependency** | None — works offline | Requires provider's server to be online |
| **Privacy** | Maximum — no tracking | Scans are logged by the provider |
| **Cost** | Free | Usually requires a paid subscription |
| **Best for** | Permanent links, business cards, print materials | Marketing campaigns, A/B testing, time-limited promotions |

## What Is a Static QR Code?

A static QR code encodes data (like a URL, text, or contact info) **directly into the QR code pattern itself**. Once generated, the data is permanently baked into the image.

### How It Works

When someone scans a static QR code, their phone reads the data pattern and opens the encoded content directly. There's no middleman, no redirect, and no server involved.

### Pros of Static QR Codes

- **Never expire** — The data lives in the image, so it works forever
- **No server dependency** — Even if the generator website goes offline, your QR code still works
- **Maximum privacy** — No scan data is collected or tracked
- **Completely free** — No recurring fees or subscriptions
- **Faster scanning** — Direct data access without URL redirects

### Cons of Static QR Codes

- **Cannot be edited** — If the destination URL changes, you need to create a new QR code and reprint
- **No analytics** — You can't track how many people scanned it
- **Larger QR patterns** — Long URLs create denser, more complex QR codes

### Best Use Cases for Static QR Codes

- **Business cards** — Your contact info or website won't change often
- **Product packaging** — Permanent link to product information
- **Restaurant menus** — Link to a stable menu URL
- **WiFi access** — Share your network credentials
- **Event signage** — Conference badges, posters, and banners

## What Is a Dynamic QR Code?

A dynamic QR code doesn't encode your actual destination URL. Instead, it encodes a **short redirect URL** (e.g., `qr.example.com/abc123`) that points to your real content. The redirect mapping is stored on the QR code provider's server.

### How It Works

1. You create a QR code → the provider gives you a short URL
2. Someone scans the code → their phone opens the short URL
3. The provider's server redirects to your actual destination
4. The provider logs the scan (time, location, device)

### Pros of Dynamic QR Codes

- **Editable** — Change the destination URL without reprinting
- **Scan analytics** — Track scan count, location, device type, time
- **Shorter redirect URLs** — Produces simpler, less dense QR patterns
- **A/B testing** — Redirect different users to different pages

### Cons of Dynamic QR Codes

- **Server-dependent** — If the provider goes offline or shuts down, your QR code breaks
- **Usually paid** — Most providers require a subscription for dynamic codes
- **Privacy concerns** — Every scan is tracked and logged
- **May expire** — Some free tiers expire dynamic codes after 30 days

### Best Use Cases for Dynamic QR Codes

- **Marketing campaigns** — Track ROI and engagement
- **Time-limited promotions** — Change the destination after the promo ends
- **A/B testing** — Split traffic between landing pages
- **Multi-channel tracking** — Compare scan rates across print, email, and in-store

## Which Should You Choose?

Here's a simple decision framework:

**Choose Static if:**
- Your content URL is permanent and won't change
- You don't need scan analytics
- You want zero ongoing costs
- Privacy matters to you or your audience
- You're printing business cards, menus, or product labels

**Choose Dynamic if:**
- You need to change the destination after printing
- You want to track scan engagement and ROI
- You're running a marketing campaign with measurable goals
- You're willing to pay a monthly subscription
